# PolTrade Website — Design & UX Specification

## 1. Project Objective

Create a polished, modern, responsive **single-page B2B website** for **PolTrade**, an independent international sales and export specialist based in Poland.

The website should present PolTrade as an experienced, hands-on commercial partner that helps manufacturers, producers, buyers and distributors develop international sales.

The website must feel:

- premium
- experienced
- personal
- commercially focused
- international
- trustworthy
- practical
- understated rather than flashy

The website should be designed as a **single-page application (SPA)** suitable for deployment as a static website on simple object storage such as an S3-style hosting environment.

This specification deliberately defines the **design, UX, content hierarchy, interactions and visual behaviour**, rather than prescribing particular technical technologies or implementation methods.

---

# 2. Core Brand Positioning

The central proposition is:

> **PolTrade does not simply advise companies about international sales. PolTrade helps execute the commercial work required to establish and develop them.**

The site should communicate practical involvement in:

- identifying suitable markets
- identifying potential customers
- contacting buyers
- presenting products
- negotiating
- establishing first orders
- developing ongoing relationships
- supporting continued international sales

The experience should communicate that PolTrade is a **hands-on commercial partner**, not a generic consultancy.

# 4. Overall Visual Direction

The website should have a strong editorial and premium B2B aesthetic.

It should combine:

- large typography
- generous whitespace
- strong visual hierarchy
- subtle borders and separators
- restrained colour usage
- carefully selected photography
- small interactive details
- a distinctive animated background element

The page should feel **designed**, rather than assembled from standard website components.

Avoid:

- generic SaaS styling
- excessive cards
- excessive rounded rectangles
- gradients everywhere
- cliché corporate imagery
- excessive icons
- overly complicated navigation
- large quantities of decorative animation
- exaggerated sales language

The design should be sophisticated enough for senior decision-makers but remain approachable and human.

---

# 5. Colour System

Use a restrained palette built around:

- a deep, almost-black primary colour
- warm/light neutral backgrounds
- white
- one distinctive accent colour associated with PolTrade

The accent colour should be used selectively for:

- primary CTAs
- active navigation
- selected options
- important links
- interactive states
- small visual highlights

Do not make the entire website brightly coloured.

Large areas of the page should remain neutral so that typography and content provide the visual hierarchy.

---

# 6. Typography

Typography is a major component of the visual identity.

Use a modern, highly legible typeface system with a clear contrast between:

### Display typography

Used for:

- hero headline
- major section headlines
- important statements

These should be large, confident and editorial.

### Supporting typography

Used for:

- section introductions
- explanatory copy
- quotes
- supporting statements

### Body typography

Used for:

- descriptions
- FAQ answers
- process explanations
- form labels

Typography should have generous spacing and comfortable reading widths.

Large headlines should be allowed to wrap naturally.

Do not compress headlines simply to keep them on one line.

---

# 7. Animated Background

A distinctive animated background should be used as part of the visual identity.

## Concept

The background represents **international movement, trade routes and connections between markets**.

It should resemble an abstract network of:

- points
- thin connecting lines
- flowing paths
- subtle geographic or logistical movement

It must remain abstract.

It should **not** attempt to reproduce a literal geographical map.

## Visual behaviour

The animation should consist of very subtle movement:

- points slowly appear and disappear
- lines gently form or shift
- small particles move along selected paths
- connections subtly change over time
- movement should feel continuous rather than repetitive

The overall effect should suggest:

**movement → connection → markets → international trade**

without literally illustrating those concepts.

## Placement

The animation should primarily appear behind the hero section and may subtly continue into selected major sections.

It should never compete with the content.

## Opacity

The background should remain sufficiently subtle that:

- all text remains highly readable
- CTAs remain visually dominant
- the visitor does not consciously need to watch the animation
- the animation acts as atmosphere rather than content

## Motion

Movement should be:

- slow
- smooth
- continuous
- understated
- non-distracting

There should be no sudden movements, flashing, rapid particle effects or dramatic transitions.

## Interaction

The animation may respond subtly to pointer movement on desktop.

For example:

- nearby points may move very slightly
- connections may respond to cursor proximity
- the network may gently shift perspective

This interaction should be extremely restrained.

It should never become a visual toy.

## Mobile

On mobile:

- simplify the animation
- reduce the number of moving elements
- reduce movement intensity
- prioritise performance and readability

## Reduced motion

When the visitor has requested reduced motion, replace the animated effect with a static version of the same abstract network.

The design should remain complete without animation.

# 31. SPA Navigation

The website should behave as a cohesive single-page experience.

Navigation should:

- scroll smoothly to sections
- account for the persistent header
- identify the currently active section where appropriate
- avoid unnecessary page transitions
- maintain sensible browser history behaviour

The user should feel as though they are moving through one continuous commercial narrative.

---

# 32. Responsive Behaviour

The website must be intentionally designed for:

- large desktop
- desktop/laptop
- tablet
- mobile

Do not simply scale down the desktop layout.

## Mobile priorities

On mobile:

- simplify navigation
- maintain access to the primary CTA
- preserve generous spacing
- shorten content line lengths
- stack complex layouts
- convert process sections to vertical flows
- convert comparisons into expandable/stacked layouts
- make contact actions prominent
- ensure comfortable form controls
- prevent horizontal scrolling

The mobile experience should feel deliberately designed rather than like a compressed desktop site.

---

# 33. Interaction States

Every interactive component should have clear states.

Define visual states for:

- default
- hover
- focus
- active
- selected
- disabled
- error
- success

Keyboard focus must be clearly visible.

Interactions should never rely solely on colour.

---

# 34. Motion

Motion throughout the website should be restrained.

Appropriate motion includes:

- smooth anchor navigation
- subtle section reveals
- gentle hover transitions
- accordion expansion
- configurator transitions
- small CTA interactions
- animated background movement

Avoid:

- dramatic page transitions
- excessive parallax
- bouncing elements
- constant motion
- long entrance animations
- animations that delay access to content

Motion should make the site feel refined, not animated for its own sake.

---

# 35. Accessibility

The design should support accessible use from the beginning.

Requirements include:

- strong colour contrast
- visible keyboard focus
- logical tab order
- meaningful button labels
- meaningful link labels
- accessible accordion controls
- accessible form labels
- clear validation messages
- no essential information communicated only through colour
- no hover-only functionality
- support for reduced motion

The website should remain fully understandable without animation.

---

# 37. Content Tone

The writing should be:

- direct
- experienced
- commercially literate
- concise
- personal
- confident

Avoid generic marketing language such as:

- "unlock your potential"
- "take your business to the next level"
- "world-class solutions"
- "innovative solutions"
- "end-to-end solutions"
- "transform your business"

Prefer concrete statements describing actual activities and outcomes.

The visitor should feel that PolTrade understands the practical reality of international sales.

**Zobacz, jak działam**

---

# 39. Performance-Oriented UX

The visual design should remain lightweight and fast.

Avoid unnecessary visual assets or interactions simply because they are technically possible.

The animated background should be particularly restrained so that it does not dominate loading or runtime performance.

The core website content must remain immediately usable even if decorative animation is unavailable.

The site should remain fully functional on slower connections.

---

# 40. Static Hosting Constraint

The website should be designed so that its core experience can be deployed as a static SPA to simple object storage.

The design must not assume a traditional server-rendered environment.

Core page content, navigation and presentation should therefore be capable of operating without a persistent application server.

Features requiring external processing — for example:

- sending contact-form submissions
- email delivery
- analytics
- assessment-result delivery

should be treated as separate services from the core website experience.

Do not allow these services to dictate the visual or UX design.

---

# 41. Overall User Journey

The entire page should tell one coherent story:

### 1. What is PolTrade?

A hands-on international sales and export partner.

### 2. Is it relevant to me?

The "Dla kogo" section allows visitors to identify their situation.

### 3. Where can PolTrade help?

The market section establishes geographical expertise.

### 4. What does PolTrade actually do?

The seven-stage process makes the work tangible.

### 5. Why should I trust PolTrade?

The founder and experience sections establish credibility.

### 6. How could we work together?

The three cooperation models explain the engagement options.

### 7. What might stop me?

The objections section addresses common concerns.

### 8. What products are relevant?

The product-category section establishes commercial breadth.

### 9. What else should I know?

The FAQ answers practical questions.

### 10. What should I do now?

The assessment and contact sections provide clear next actions.

---

# 42. Design Success Criteria

The final design should pass the following test.

A new visitor should be able to answer these questions quickly:

1. What does PolTrade do?
2. Who does PolTrade work with?
3. Which markets does PolTrade cover?
4. What does PolTrade actually do for its clients?
5. What happens during the engagement?
6. Why is PolTrade credible?
7. What are the available cooperation models?
8. What are the main risks or objections?
9. Can PolTrade help with my type of product?
10. How can I determine which model is appropriate?
11. How can I contact PolTrade?

The page should feel like a **well-structured commercial conversation**, not a catalogue of services.

The final impression should be:

> **An experienced international sales professional who understands the market, has relationships with buyers, and is prepared to do the commercial work.**

The visual design, content hierarchy, animation and interactions should all reinforce that impression.
